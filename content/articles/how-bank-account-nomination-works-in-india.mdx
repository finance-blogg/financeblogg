Nomination is the instruction you give a bank: if I die, **this person** is the one you may pay the balance to, so the family does not have to start with a long legal file. It is one of the cheapest pieces of paperwork in personal finance, and many accounts still have none.

This article covers savings accounts, FDs and similar bank deposits. Safe-deposit lockers and small-savings schemes at the post office follow their own forms.

<KeyTakeaways>
- A **nominee** is the person the bank is told to pay on the depositor’s death, subject to the bank’s rules and the law.
- Nomination is **not** the same as a will. It is a settlement convenience for the bank, not a full succession plan.
- You can usually **add, change or cancel** a nomination while you are alive, using the bank’s form or app.
- Joint accounts, minors as nominees, and multiple nominees have extra fields; fill them, do not assume.
- Keep the **acknowledgement**. A nomination you cannot prove is a nomination the survivor may have to argue about.
</KeyTakeaways>

## What nomination is for

When an account holder dies, the bank must decide whom it can safely pay. Without nomination, many banks ask for a legal heir certificate, a succession certificate, or a stamped indemnity, depending on the amount and the bank’s deceased-claim policy.

A registered nomination lets the bank pay the **nominee** on production of the death certificate and the nominee’s KYC, along with the bank’s claim form. That is faster for ordinary balances. It does not mean other heirs lose every right in court; it means the bank has a named payee.

<Callout type="note">
Paying the nominee settles the **bank’s** obligation on that deposit. Disputes among family members about who ultimately owns the money are a separate legal question. If the estate is large or contested, a will and legal advice matter more than a tick-box on a savings form.
</Callout>

## Nominee vs joint holder vs legal heir

| Role | What it means while you are alive | What it means after death |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Joint holder (either or survivor) | Can operate the account as the mandate allows | Often continues; read the operating instruction |
| Nominee | Usually **no** operating rights | Bank may pay this person the balance |
| Legal heir | No automatic bank access | Claims under succession law, with documents |

Do not add a relative as a joint holder only because you wanted a nominee. Joint holding is a live access right. Nomination is a death instruction.

## Who can be a nominee

Banks generally allow an individual. A **minor** can be a nominee if you name a guardian to receive the money until the minor becomes an adult. Some products now allow **more than one nominee** with shares; if your form has percentage fields, use them so the bank is not guessing.

You typically **cannot** name a trust, a company or “my family” in one line. Use a person, and keep the name identical to their KYC.

For Hindu undivided families, partnership accounts and similar non-individual forms, nomination rules differ. Ask the branch for the form that matches the account type.

## How to add or change a nomination

Do it when you open the account, then again whenever the family changes.

1. Use the bank’s **nomination form** (often DA1 for deposits) or the nomination option in net banking or the app.
2. Fill the nominee’s name, address, relationship, date of birth if a minor, and guardian if needed.
3. Sign as the account holder. For joint accounts, the bank will say who must sign.
4. Collect a **written or in-app acknowledgement**. RBI has told banks to acknowledge nominations; keep a screenshot or PDF.
5. Repeat for **every** FD, RD, savings account and demat-linked bank account you care about. Nomination on one product does not copy to the rest.

Change the nominee after marriage, divorce, a death in the family, or a falling-out. Cancellation uses a separate form. Do not rely on a WhatsApp message to the RM.

## What the nominee should keep

- Account numbers and IFSC, not only the debit card.
- The nomination acknowledgement, if they have a copy.
- Your PAN and a way to find the death certificate when the time comes.

The nominee should also know that [deposit insurance](/blog/what-deposit-insurance-covers-in-india) is about the bank failing, not about nomination. Nomination does not increase the DICGC ceiling.

## Where nomination is not enough

- **Wills and gifts** still matter for houses, jewellery and anything not in that bank.
- **Insurance** has its own nomination under insurance law.
- **Mutual funds and demat** have separate nominee fields on the folio or NSDL/CDSL record.
- A nominee who is not a legal heir may still face a family claim. Do not use nomination as a secret will to cut someone out without legal advice.

If a bank delays a straightforward nominated claim, the customer (or nominee) can use RBI’s complaint system after the bank’s own grievance channel.

We think every adult should spend one afternoon listing accounts and confirming nominees, the same way they check a [bank statement](/blog/how-to-read-a-bank-statement). It is dull. It is also the difference between a week of forms and a year of affidavits.
</Callout>

## A short checklist

1. Savings and salary accounts: nominee named, acknowledgement saved.
2. FDs and [RDs](/blog/how-recurring-deposits-work): nomination on each, or a bank process that clearly covers them.
3. Joint operating instruction matches what you intend (survivor vs joint).
4. Nominee KYC name matches Aadhaar and PAN.
5. After any life event, update; do not wait for the next branch visit “when convenient”.
