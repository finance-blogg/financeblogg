Before you file an income tax return, the department already holds a file about you. Form **26AS** is the tax-credit statement: who deducted tax on your behalf, advance tax you paid, and certain other entries. The **Annual Information Statement (AIS)** is a wider report of information that banks, employers, registrars and others have sent in: salary, interest, securities, property, and specified high-value items.

Reading both is less about software and more about catching a mismatch while you can still explain it. This is a companion to our [income tax basics](/blog/basics-of-income-tax-in-india) guide, not a replacement for the ITR form instructions.

<KeyTakeaways>
- **26AS** is mainly about **tax already paid or deducted** (TDS, TCS, advance tax, self-assessment tax) and related credits.
- **AIS** is a broader information statement. **TIS** (Taxpayer Information Summary) is a processed, de-duplicated view of AIS.
- If 26AS does not show TDS that your payslip claims, your refund or tax due will be wrong until it is fixed.
- AIS can list transactions you forgot. That does not always mean the whole amount is taxable income.
- GST returns are a **different portal**. Do not look for GSTR-2B inside 26AS.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Where these statements live

They are generated on the Income Tax **e-filing** portal after you log in with PAN. The department pulls data from deductors (your employer, bank, buyer of a property) who file their TDS returns.

There is a lag. A January salary credited in February, or a bank that filed late, can show up after you expected it. If you download 26AS on 1 April, it is not a picture of the whole year yet.

<Callout type="verify">
Menu names and whether the law refers to a “financial year”, “assessment year” or “tax year” can change. Use the portal’s current labels and the year selector at the top of the statement. India’s Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026; confirm terminology on [incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in).
</Callout>

## Form 26AS: the credit ledger

Read 26AS as an answer to: **whose tax credit is in my PAN, and is it enough?**

Typical parts (the portal’s exact part names can move):

- **TDS on salary**, with the employer’s TAN and the amount of tax deducted.
- **TDS on other income**, such as bank interest, rent if you are a deductee, or payments to professionals.
- **TCS**, if a seller collected tax at source on a specified purchase.
- **Advance tax and self-assessment tax** challans you paid.
- **Refunds** already issued, in some views.
- **Specified transactions** that used to appear more prominently here; much of that detail now also (or mainly) lives in AIS.

Check three numbers against your own records:

1. **Salary TDS** vs the Form 16 your employer issued.
2. **Bank and other TDS** vs interest certificates and Form 16A.
3. **Challan amounts and CINs** vs what you actually paid.

If Form 16 says ₹80,000 TDS and 26AS shows ₹60,000, do not file as if the ₹80,000 were already with the department. Ask the employer to correct their TDS return. Filing first and arguing later is how refunds stall.

## AIS: the wider information report

AIS is closer to: **what have reporting entities said you did?**

It may include salary, dividend, interest, securities transactions, mutual fund purchases or redemptions as reported, property, foreign remittance information, and other specified items. The list of reportable items is set by the department and can grow.

AIS is **information**. It is not an assessment order. A reported mutual fund purchase is not a tax bill. A reported property purchase is a prompt to check whether you have a corresponding source of funds and, if you sold, whether capital gains are in the return.

The portal lets you **give feedback** on AIS rows (confirm, mark as duplicate, mark as incorrect, and similar options). Use that when a row is clearly wrong, not as a way to hide income.

**TIS** then summarises AIS after the department’s processing. Prefill in the ITR often leans on this chain. If you ignore AIS and the prefill, you may still be asked later why a reported item is missing.

<Callout type="example" title="Interest you forgot">
Illustrative. You closed an [RD](/blog/how-recurring-deposits-work) and forgot the interest. The bank reported interest and may have deducted TDS. AIS shows the interest; 26AS shows the TDS. If you omit the interest in the ITR, the TDS credit may not sit cleanly, and the department already has the interest figure.
</Callout>

## A practical reading order

1. Pick the correct **year** on the portal.
2. Download **26AS** and list every TAN and challan.
3. Open **AIS**, then **TIS**, and tick each row against Form 16, bank statements, broker reports and sale deeds.
4. For every TDS line, confirm the **income** is also in your computation.
5. For AIS rows that are not income (some purchases), note them so you are not surprised by a later query.
6. Only then start the ITR, using prefill as a helper, not as a substitute for your own totals.

Our article on [reading a bank statement](/blog/how-to-read-a-bank-statement) helps you find interest credits that should appear somewhere in this set.

## Common mismatches

- **Employer TAN error** or a late TDS return: Form 16 and 26AS disagree.
- **Joint accounts:** interest may be reported against one PAN. Decide the taxable share with records, not guesswork.
- **Old employer** still using your PAN after you left.
- **Wrong PAN** given to a bank or buyer; someone else’s credit, or yours missing.
- **GST vs income tax:** a freelance invoice may involve GST on the GST portal and TDS or advance tax on the income-tax side. They do not cancel automatically.

<Callout type="warning" title="Do not buy a “26AS repair” from a stranger">
A person on a messaging app who asks for your OTP to “fix 26AS” is not a department employee. Use the official portal, your employer’s payroll team, or a professional you appointed. The e-filing site will not call you to demand a secret code.
</Callout>

## What 26AS is not

It is not a GST return, not a complete list of your spending, and not proof that you owe zero tax. You can have no TDS and still owe tax. You can have TDS and still owe more if your slab tax is higher than what was deducted.

It also does not replace [goal planning](/blog/how-to-set-financial-goals-in-india). Tax credits are history. A budget is the next twelve months.

<Callout type="opinion">
We think the 15 quiet minutes with 26AS and AIS in June save more grief than any last-day ITR video. The return is a story that has to match the department’s file. Read their file first.
</Callout>

If a row is wrong and the deductor will not revise, follow the department’s correction process and keep emails. If you need professional help, a Chartered Accountant or tax practitioner you chose is the route, not an unverified “refund maximiser”.
