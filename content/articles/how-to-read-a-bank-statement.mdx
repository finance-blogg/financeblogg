A bank statement is the most complete record of your money. It shows every rupee that entered and left your account during a period, along with charges, interest and automatic debits you might not notice otherwise. Reading it carefully once a month takes a few minutes and can catch errors, forgotten subscriptions and fraud early.

The layout differs between banks, but the building blocks are the same.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Every statement has a header with your account details, a summary of balances and a list of transactions.
- The **value date** is when money is treated as moved for interest purposes; it can differ from the transaction date.
- Transaction descriptions use short codes such as UPI, NEFT, IMPS, NACH and POS. The exact format varies by bank.
- Check for unknown debits, duplicate charges, recurring payments you no longer want and bank fees.
- Report unauthorised transactions to your bank **immediately**. Under RBI rules, how quickly you report can affect how much of the loss you bear.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Where to get your statement

You can usually download a statement for any date range from your bank's internet banking or mobile app, typically as a PDF. Many banks also email a monthly statement, often as a password-protected PDF. You can request a printed statement at the branch, and some accounts still use a passbook that is updated at the branch or at a passbook printing kiosk.

## The header: your account details

The top of the statement usually shows:

- **Account holder name and address.**
- **Account number**, often partly masked for safety.
- **Account type**, such as savings or current.
- **Branch name, IFSC and sometimes MICR code.** The IFSC identifies your branch for NEFT, RTGS and IMPS transfers.
- **Customer ID or CIF number**, the bank's identifier for you as a customer.
- **Statement period**, the start and end dates covered.

Some banks also show whether a nominee is registered for the account. If yours shows no nominee, consider adding one through your bank.

## The summary: opening and closing balance

Most statements include a summary like this:

**Opening balance + total credits − total debits = closing balance**

If you are reconciling your own records, this is the first check: the closing balance of one statement should equal the opening balance of the next.

## The transaction table

Each row is one transaction. The columns typically include:

| Column | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Date or transaction date | When the transaction was made or recorded |
| Value date | The date from which the bank treats the money as debited or credited, for interest purposes |
| Narration, description or particulars | What the transaction was, in short-code form |
| Cheque or reference number | A cheque number, or a reference number for electronic transfers |
| Withdrawal or debit | Money leaving your account |
| Deposit or credit | Money entering your account |
| Balance | Your running balance after the transaction |

**Transaction date vs value date.** Usually these match. They can differ, for example, when a cheque you deposit takes time to clear, or when a transaction made on a holiday is processed on the next working day. Savings interest is calculated on balances by value date.

## Decoding transaction descriptions

The narration is where most confusion arises. Banks abbreviate heavily, and formats vary. The table below shows common elements you are likely to see. **These are examples, not a universal standard.** If you cannot work out an entry, ask your bank.

| You may see | Usually means |
| --- | --- |
| UPI/…/name/… | A UPI payment or receipt, often with the other party's name or UPI ID and a reference number |
| NEFT, RTGS | Bank transfers through the National Electronic Funds Transfer or Real Time Gross Settlement systems |
| IMPS | An Immediate Payment Service transfer |
| ATM WDL, ATW, CASH WDL | A cash withdrawal at an ATM |
| POS, PUR | A debit card purchase at a shop's card machine |
| ECOM, ONLINE | An online debit card purchase |
| NACH, ACH, ECS | An automatic debit or credit through a mandate, such as a loan EMI, SIP, insurance premium or salary |
| SI | A standing instruction you set up with your bank |
| INT, INT.PD, CREDIT INTEREST | Interest credited to your account |
| TDS | Tax deducted at source, typically on deposit interest |
| CHRG, CHG, FEE | A bank charge, for example for SMS alerts, a debit card annual fee, a cheque book or not maintaining the minimum balance |
| REV, RVSL | A reversal, such as a refund of a failed transaction |
| CLG, CHQ | A cheque that was cleared |

## Monthly checks worth doing

1. **Unknown transactions.** Any debit you do not recognise needs to be explained. Search your UPI app, email and card alerts first; if you still cannot match it, contact your bank.
2. **Duplicates.** Two identical debits close together may be a genuine double payment or a processing error.
3. **Failed payments.** If a UPI or card payment failed but money was debited, it should normally be reversed automatically. RBI has set time limits for these reversals and compensation if banks are late. If the reversal does not appear, raise a complaint with your bank.
4. **Automatic debits.** Review NACH, SI and card subscriptions. Cancel mandates you no longer need through your bank or the merchant.
5. **Bank charges.** Look for minimum balance penalties, cheque return charges or fees you did not expect, and ask about any that seem wrong.
6. **Interest and TDS.** Check interest credits and any TDS. These will also appear in your Annual Information Statement for tax purposes.

The same review feeds your budget. Our guide on [creating a monthly budget](/blog/how-to-create-a-monthly-budget-in-india) shows how to group statement entries into categories.

## If you see an unauthorised transaction

Act quickly:

1. **Block** your card or UPI access through your bank's app, website or helpline.
2. **Report** the transaction to your bank immediately and keep the complaint reference number.
3. **Report cyber fraud** by calling the national helpline **1930** or filing a complaint on the [National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal](https://cybercrime.gov.in).

RBI rules on unauthorised electronic transactions link a customer's liability to how quickly the fraud is reported and whether the customer was negligent, for example by sharing an OTP or PIN. In broad terms, if the fraud resulted from a breach at the bank's or a third party's end and you report it within three working days, you should not bear the loss. Delayed reporting can increase your liability. If your bank does not resolve your complaint, you can escalate it to the RBI Ombudsman through the [RBI Complaint Management System](https://cms.rbi.org.in).

<Callout type="warning">
Banks will never ask for your OTP, PIN, CVV or password by phone, SMS, email or messaging app. Do not share these with anyone, including people claiming to be from your bank, the RBI or the police.
</Callout>

## Keeping your statements safe

- Statements contain personal and financial information. Store PDFs securely and do not forward the password together with the file.
- When you share a statement, for a loan or visa application for example, share it only with the organisation that needs it, through official channels.
- Be wary of apps that ask you to upload bank statements in exchange for loans or "credit checks", especially unregulated lending apps.
