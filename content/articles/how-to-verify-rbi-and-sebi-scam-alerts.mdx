Fake messages that look like **RBI** or **SEBI** alerts travel faster than real ones. They borrow logos, imitate circular language and create panic: “Your account will be frozen”, “SEBI-approved recovery agent”, “Guaranteed returns—act today”. The defence is not memorising every scam. It is knowing **where truth lives** and refusing to act on urgency alone.

This explainer teaches a verification habit for Indian readers. It is not an exhaustive scam database.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Real RBI and SEBI communications are published on **official websites**; start there, not in WhatsApp PDFs.
- Regulators do not ask for your OTP, CVV, net-banking password or remote screen control to “verify” you.
- Cross-check entity names in SEBI’s and RBI’s public lists before transferring money to “advisors” or “apps”.
- Treat unexpected links, APK files and “refund” forms as hostile until proven otherwise.
- When unsure, stop, verify on the official domain you typed yourself, then call your bank using the number on the back of your card or the bank’s website.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Why fake alerts work

Scammers combine three levers:

1. **Authority** — RBI, SEBI, police, courier or “KYC department” branding.  
2. **Fear or greed** — frozen accounts, legal cases, or “guaranteed” market profits.  
3. **Speed** — you must act in minutes, before you can think.

Your job is to break the speed lever. No genuine regulatory process requires you to share secrets in a five-minute phone call.

## Step 1: Open the official site yourself

Type the address carefully or use a bookmark:

- RBI: [https://www.rbi.org.in](https://www.rbi.org.in)  
- SEBI: [https://www.sebi.gov.in](https://www.sebi.gov.in)  
- SEBI investor education: [https://investor.sebi.gov.in](https://investor.sebi.gov.in)

Then search press releases, circulars or investor alerts for the claim you saw. If the sensational message has **no matching official page**, treat the forward as false until proven.

The same habit helps when reading real policy news—see [how to read an RBI monetary policy statement](/blog/how-to-read-an-rbi-monetary-policy-statement) and [how to read SEBI mutual fund circulars](/blog/how-to-read-sebi-mutual-fund-circulars).

## Step 2: Check the channel

| Claim channel | Healthy response |
| --- | --- |
| WhatsApp / Telegram broadcast | Verify on official site; do not tap shortened links |
| Phone call demanding OTP | Hang up; call bank from official number |
| Email with urgent attachment | Open website separately; never enable macros |
| App asking for screen sharing | Refuse; banks do not need TeamViewer-style access for KYC |
| “Recovery agent” after a scam | Contact bank/police cyber channels; beware second-layer fraud |

Our banking KYC guide covers related tricks: [what KYC means for bank accounts](/blog/what-is-kyc-for-bank-accounts-in-india).

<Callout type="warning" title="OTP is the key to your money">
Anyone who asks you to read an OTP, PIN or CVV is asking for control of your account. Regulators and banks will not request these over phone or chat to “complete verification”.
</Callout>

## Step 3: Verify the person or app, not the logo

Before you invest or share data:

- Confirm whether a securities market intermediary is registered using SEBI’s official public tools and lists on sebi.gov.in / investor.sebi.gov.in.  
- Confirm whether a bank or payment claim matches RBI’s published information—not a lookalike domain (extra letters, “.co”, misspellings).  
- Prefer installing apps from official store listings linked from the institution’s own website.

Screenshots of “certificates” are trivial to forge. Registration lookups are harder to fake when you perform them yourself.

## Step 4: Separate education from tips

FinanceBlogg publishes explainers. It does not send stock tips, recovery offers or KYC OTPs. SEBI repeatedly warns against unregistered tipsters and guaranteed-return schemes. If a message mixes a real circular headline with a private “join this paid group” pitch, the pitch is the scam payload.

When small-savings or policy numbers appear in forwards, verify against primary sources the way we describe for [small savings rate revisions](/blog/how-to-read-small-savings-interest-rate-revisions) and [Union Budget reading for households](/blog/how-to-read-union-budget-for-households).

## Step 5: If you already shared something

1. Call your bank’s official helpline immediately; ask to block cards/UPI/net banking as needed.  
2. Change passwords from a safe device.  
3. Note transaction references; use your bank’s fraud reporting path.  
4. Report on the government’s cybercrime channels as applicable.  
5. Do not pay a “recovery fee” to strangers who message you next.

## A one-minute checklist

- Did I type the official domain myself?  
- Does a matching press release or circular exist today?  
- Is anyone asking for OTP, remote access or advance fees?  
- Can I wait 24 hours without losing anything real?  
- Would I make this transfer if the logo were removed from the message?

If any answer fails, pause.

## Limitations

Scam tactics evolve. This article teaches verification methods, not every active fraud. Official URLs and helplines can change; always prefer the contact details published on the regulator’s or bank’s current website. FinanceBlogg cannot freeze accounts or recover funds for you.

## Bottom line

Believe RBI and SEBI alerts only after you find them on official websites you opened yourself. Ignore urgency, protect OTPs, and verify intermediaries before money moves. Slow verification is cheaper than fast regret.
