Your [credit score](/blog/how-credit-scores-work-in-india) is built from data in your **credit information report**. If that report is wrong—an old loan still shown as open, a card you never took, a settlement marked incorrectly—your score and loan applications can suffer. This guide explains how to read the report carefully and raise a dispute. It is not a guarantee that every dispute will succeed.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Pull your report from each credit information company you use and check **account status, balances, dates and personal details**.
- RBI frameworks give individuals rights around access to credit information; confirm the current free-report rules on official sources.
- Disputes usually go to the **credit information company**, which then checks with the **lender** that reported the data.
- Keep written proof: statements, closure letters, NOCs and payment receipts.
</KeyTakeaways>

## What to check on the report

When you open a report, verify:

- Name, date of birth, PAN, addresses and phone numbers.
- Each loan or card: lender name, account type, open/closed status, overdue amount, write-off or settled flags.
- Enquiry (hard search) entries you do not recognise.
- Ownership: individual vs joint / authorised user, if shown.

A lower score is not automatically an “error”. Missed payments and high utilisation can be accurate and still painful.

## Common error patterns

- Loan or card closed in the bank’s system but still **active** on the report.
- Amount overdue after you have already paid.
- Duplicate accounts.
- Someone else’s account linked because of mixed identity data (rare, but serious).
- Old address or wrong employment details cluttering the file.

<Callout type="warning" title="Fraud vs reporting delay">
If you see an account you never opened, treat it as urgent: contact the lender and the credit information company, and consider filing a police complaint if fraud is involved. A short reporting lag after a recent payment is different from a fake account.
</Callout>

## Basic dispute steps

1. **Download** the latest full report (not only a score number from an ad).
2. **Mark** each wrong line with what you believe is correct.
3. **Gather proof**: bank statements, foreclosure letter, settlement letter, identity documents.
4. **Raise a dispute** through the credit information company’s official website or app process.
5. **Track** the complaint number and follow up until you see a written outcome.
6. **Re-check** a fresh report after the correction is confirmed.

Exact screens and forms differ across Experian, Equifax, CRIF High Mark, TransUnion CIBIL and others. Use their official portals only.

## What usually does not work

- Asking a friend to “fix the score” informally.
- Paying a random agent who promises a higher score in days.
- Ignoring lender communication while disputing—many corrections need the lender to update the feed.

## After the correction

Keep a PDF of the corrected report. If you are applying for a loan, give the lender time to see the updated file. For borrowing concepts more broadly, read how [loan interest rates](/blog/understanding-loan-interest-rates) and [loan EMIs](/blog/how-loan-emi-is-calculated) work.

<Callout type="verify">
Free access rules, dispute timelines and compensation frameworks for credit information issues are set under RBI directions and can change. Confirm the current position on [rbi.org.in](https://www.rbi.org.in) and on your credit information company’s help pages.
</Callout>
