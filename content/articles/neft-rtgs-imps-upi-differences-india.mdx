Indian banks offer several ways to send money electronically. The names sound similar, but **NEFT**, **RTGS**, **IMPS** and **UPI** are different systems with different typical uses. Knowing the difference helps you pick a suitable channel and read a [bank statement](/blog/how-to-read-a-bank-statement) with less confusion.

This article explains the ideas. Exact limits, charges and cut-offs depend on your bank and can change—confirm in your bank’s schedule of charges and RBI/NPCI pages.

<KeyTakeaways>
- **UPI** is designed for everyday person-to-person and merchant payments using a VPA or QR, usually through an app.
- **IMPS** is an immediate interbank transfer option available through net banking and apps, often identified by account + IFSC or MMID.
- **NEFT** is a widely used bank transfer system for sending money to another account using account number and IFSC.
- **RTGS** is typically used for **larger** value transfers that settle in real time between banks, subject to RBI’s minimum amount rules.
</KeyTakeaways>

## UPI in everyday language

UPI (Unified Payments Interface) links bank accounts through a payment address (VPA) or QR code. It is built for frequent, smaller everyday payments: splitting a bill, paying a shop, sending money to family.

If a UPI debit succeeds on your side but the recipient does not get credit, follow your bank’s or app’s official dispute / complaint flow and keep the UTR or reference number.

## IMPS

IMPS (Immediate Payment Service) is an NPCI service for immediate credit, available through channels your bank enables (mobile banking, net banking and sometimes ATMs). You typically need the beneficiary’s account details (and IFSC) or other identifiers your bank supports.

People often use IMPS when they want an immediate bank-to-bank transfer without using a UPI ID.

## NEFT

NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer) is an RBI system for transferring funds between bank accounts using account number and IFSC. It is common for rent, fees, vendor payments and other transfers where you are comfortable with bank-processed settlement rather than a QR scan.

NEFT availability and processing windows have been widened over the years. Always check your bank’s current NEFT timings and any amount caps for online initiation.

## RTGS

RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement) is meant for **high-value** transfers that settle individually in real time between banks. RBI sets a **minimum** amount for RTGS customer transactions; banks may set their own upper limits for online channels.

If you are moving a large sum (for example, a property-related payment), your bank may guide you toward RTGS or another high-value channel. Confirm the minimum amount and cut-offs on the day you transfer.

<Callout type="verify">
Minimum amounts, operating hours and features for NEFT, RTGS, IMPS and UPI are set or enabled under RBI and NPCI frameworks and can change. Read the current FAQs on [rbi.org.in](https://www.rbi.org.in) and [npci.org.in](https://www.npci.org.in) before relying on a specific number or timing.
</Callout>

## A practical way to choose

| Need | Often suitable starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Coffee, groceries, splitting a bill | UPI |
| Immediate transfer using account + IFSC | IMPS (if enabled) |
| Standard account-to-account payment | NEFT |
| Large-value bank transfer | RTGS (if above the minimum) |

This table is a learning aid, not a rulebook. Your bank’s app may label products differently or bundle them under “Fund transfer”.

## Safety habits that apply to all of them

- Verify the **account number / IFSC / UPI ID** before confirming—reversals are not guaranteed.
- Prefer official bank or known UPI apps; avoid links from unknown messages.
- Keep SMS/email alerts on for debits.
- For large transfers, do a small test credit first when practical.

<Callout type="warning" title="Never share OTPs or PINs">
No genuine bank officer needs your UPI PIN, net-banking password or OTP to “complete” a credit to your account.
</Callout>
