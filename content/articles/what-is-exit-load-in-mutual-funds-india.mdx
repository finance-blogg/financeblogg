When you redeem mutual fund units, the amount you receive is based on the applicable **Net Asset Value (NAV)**—see [what NAV means](/blog/what-is-nav-in-mutual-funds-india)—minus any charges the scheme rules allow. One such charge is an **exit load**: a fee some schemes deduct if you redeem within a stated period after purchase.

Exit load is a scheme feature, not a universal tax and not the same as the annual [expense ratio](/blog/mutual-fund-expense-ratio-explained-india).

<KeyTakeaways>
- An exit load is a **redemption charge** that may apply if you sell units before a holding period defined in the scheme documents.
- Rates and time windows differ by scheme—and can differ for SIP instalments bought on different dates.
- Exit load ≠ expense ratio ≠ capital gains tax; all three can affect what you keep.
- Always verify the current load in the Scheme Information Document (SID), Key Information Memorandum (KIM) or factsheet.
- Planning redemptions around goals reduces accidental exit-load costs—especially before [rebalancing](/blog/what-portfolio-rebalancing-means-for-beginners).
</KeyTakeaways>

## Why schemes use exit loads

Fund houses may use exit loads to discourage very short-term round trips that raise trading costs for remaining investors. Whether a particular scheme charges a load, and for how long, is a product design choice disclosed up front.

Some schemes charge **nil** exit load. Others charge a percentage if you redeem within, for example, a set number of days from allotment. Never assume the rule you remember from another fund applies to this one.

## How the charge is usually calculated

In plain terms:

1. You place a redemption for a number of units or an amount.  
2. If those units are still inside the exit-load window, the registrar applies the disclosed percentage to the applicable value.  
3. You receive the net amount after the load (and after any tax the law requires the fund or you to handle separately).

For SIPs, each instalment can have its **own** clock. Redeeming “some units” may pull a mix of old (load-free) and new (still within load) units, depending on the fund’s FIFO or other unit-allocation method described in the documents.

<Callout type="example" title="Illustrative exit load">
Teaching example only—not any real scheme’s rule.

Suppose a scheme charges **1% exit load** if units are redeemed within 365 days. You redeem units worth ₹50,000 that are 200 days old. An illustrative load of 1% would be ₹500, so the pre-tax proceeds concept starts from about ₹49,500 before other adjustments.

If the same units were 400 days old and the load window had ended, that particular load would not apply.
</Callout>

## Where to read the real numbers

Do not rely on memory or a friend’s WhatsApp summary. Check:

- the **factsheet** cost section—our guide: [how to read a mutual fund factsheet](/blog/how-to-read-a-mutual-fund-factsheet);
- the **SID / KIM** for the legal description of loads;
- the fund house or registrar transaction preview before you confirm a redemption.

Loads can be revised for *future* investments as per regulatory process; units you already hold follow the rules that apply to them as disclosed.

## Exit load vs expense ratio vs tax

| Item | What it is | When you feel it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expense ratio / TER | Ongoing fund running cost reflected in NAV | Continuously while you stay invested |
| Exit load | Possible fee on early redemption | Only if you redeem inside the window |
| Capital gains tax | Tax on profits under income-tax law | When gains are taxable on sale / as per law |

Paying attention to only one of these three paints an incomplete cost picture. Direct vs regular plans change expense ratios, not the basic idea of exit load—see [direct vs regular plans](/blog/direct-vs-regular-mutual-fund-plans).

## Practical habits

- Match fund liquidity to your goal: money needed in three months may not belong in a scheme with a long exit-load window.  
- Before switching funds for performance chasing, add exit load plus tax plus new costs to the story.  
- For SIPs, check which instalments will attract load if you redeem partially.  
- Keep an emergency fund outside market funds so you are not forced to redeem at a bad time *and* pay a load.

[SIP investing basics](/blog/understanding-sip-and-mutual-fund-investing) still apply: time in a suitable fund matters more than frequent exits.

## Limitations

Exit-load percentages, slabs and exemptions vary by scheme and can change for new investments under disclosed rules. This article does not list live loads for any fund and is not a recommendation to buy, hold or redeem. Confirm details in official scheme documents and on [investor.sebi.gov.in](https://investor.sebi.gov.in) / [amfiindia.com](https://www.amfiindia.com).

## Bottom line

Exit load is an early-redemption charge some mutual funds apply for a defined period. Read it before you invest and again before you redeem, and separate it mentally from expense ratios and taxes so your cost picture stays honest.
