A **No-Claim Bonus (NCB)** in health insurance is a benefit many retail policies offer when you complete a policy year **without a claim** (as defined in the wording). The bonus may increase **sum insured**, offer a **premium discount**, or take another form stated in the product—there is no single universal NCB design.

This article helps you read the feature. It is not a recommendation to buy or switch any policy.

<KeyTakeaways>
- NCB rewards **claim-free continuity** under the policy’s own definition of a claim.
- Benefits are **capped** and structured differently across products—read the prospectus and policy.
- Making a claim can **reduce or reset** NCB under stated rules; do not assume “one small claim changes nothing”.
- NCB is only one line when you [compare policies](/blog/how-to-compare-health-insurance-policies); waiting periods and exclusions still dominate claim outcomes.
</KeyTakeaways>

## What insurers usually mean by NCB

In many retail health products, if no eligible claim is paid (or sometimes, no claim is intimated) in a policy year, the insurer adds a bonus for the next year. Common educational patterns include:

- a percentage increase in **sum insured** at renewal without a matching rise in base premium for that bonus layer; or  
- a **discount** on renewal premium; or  
- a product-specific alternative described in the brochure.

Always treat marketing tables as **product-specific**, not industry law.

## What counts as a “claim” for NCB

Wording matters. Ask:

1. Does a **settled** claim affect NCB, or also an intimated-but-withdrawn claim?  
2. Do **cashless** and **reimbursement** claims both count?  
3. Do claims only for certain benefits (for example, some wellness add-ons) sit outside NCB rules?  
4. How do **family floater** claims affect the bonus for the policy?

If the customer-care chat answer differs from the policy PDF, trust the PDF.

<Callout type="verify">
IRDAI product regulations and insurer wordings evolve. Confirm NCB accumulation, maximum bonus and reduction-after-claim rules in the latest policy document for your product, not in a generic blog table.
</Callout>

## Limits and fine print people miss

- **Maximum NCB** — bonuses usually stop growing after a cap.  
- **Restoration / recharge** benefits are separate from NCB.  
- **Bonus sum insured** may have conditions (for example, applicability to base cover only).  
- Switching insurers can involve **portability** rules; NCB transfer is not something to assume without reading both policies and IRDAI portability provisions.

Waiting periods and exclusions still decide whether a hospitalisation is payable at all—see [waiting periods and exclusions](/blog/health-insurance-waiting-periods-and-exclusions). Cost-sharing features such as [deductible and co-pay](/blog/health-insurance-deductible-copay-india) also change your out-of-pocket cost independent of NCB.

## Should you avoid claiming “to protect NCB”?

That is a household judgement, not a slogan. A large unavoidable hospital bill usually matters more than protecting a bonus percentage. A tiny bill near year-end might be weighed against NCB impact **only after** you understand your policy’s reduction rules and your cash buffer.

Never delay emergency care to protect a bonus.

## Checklist when reading an NCB table

1. Is NCB an **increase in cover** or a **premium discount**?  
2. What is the **maximum** bonus?  
3. What happens to NCB **after a claim**?  
4. Does the bonus apply to **all** benefits under the policy?  
5. How does NCB interact with **zone**, room-rent limits and sub-limits?

Write the answers into your comparison sheet beside network hospitals and exclusions.

<Callout type="warning" title="Sales pitches">
“Guaranteed bonus every year forever” is incomplete without caps and claim-reset rules. Ask for the policy wording page, not only the flyer.
</Callout>

## Bottom line

No-Claim Bonus is a **continuity feature**, not a substitute for adequate sum insured, clear waiting periods or honest disclosure at proposal. Read it as one column in a larger policy review.
