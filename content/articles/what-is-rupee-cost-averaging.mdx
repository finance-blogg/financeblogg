**Rupee cost averaging** (RCA) describes what happens when you invest a **fixed rupee amount** at regular intervals into a market-linked product whose **price per unit changes**. When prices are lower, the same rupees buy more units; when prices are higher, they buy fewer units.

It is a description of arithmetic—not a guarantee of profit, and not advice to start or stop a [SIP](/blog/understanding-sip-and-mutual-fund-investing).

<KeyTakeaways>
- RCA is the unit-buying effect of investing **fixed amounts on a schedule** while prices move.
- It can reduce the need to guess a single “perfect” entry day; it does **not** remove market risk.
- A falling market can still produce losses; averaging is not insurance.
- Compare the idea with [diversification](/blog/what-diversification-means-for-beginner-investors) and [asset allocation](/blog/what-asset-allocation-means-for-beginners)—they answer different questions.
</KeyTakeaways>

## A simple picture

Imagine you invest ₹5,000 every month into a fund whose NAV changes.

<Callout type="example" title="Illustrative units (not a real fund)">
Month 1: NAV ₹50 → 100 units. Month 2: NAV ₹40 → 125 units. Month 3: NAV ₹50 → 100 units. You invested ₹15,000 and hold 325 units. Average cost per unit is ₹15,000 ÷ 325 ≈ ₹46.15. These figures are invented for teaching.
</Callout>

Your average purchase price sits between the highs and lows of the path you happened to buy on. A different path would produce a different average.

## What RCA does not do

- It does not promise that your average cost will be below the future selling price.
- It does not protect you if the investment stays down for years.
- It does not make a poor product into a good one.
- It does not replace understanding risk, time horizon or [how inflation affects savings](/blog/how-inflation-affects-your-savings).

## RCA and SIPs

In India, people often meet RCA through mutual fund SIPs: a fixed debit buys units at each instalment’s NAV. The SIP is the **instruction**; rupee cost averaging is the **unit maths** that follows when NAVs change.

You can also see similar arithmetic with other scheduled investments. The label matters less than understanding volatility and your time horizon.

## Lump sum vs regular investing (conceptually)

A lump sum invested on one day gets one purchase price. Regular investing gets many. Neither approach is automatically “better” for every person and every market path. Education materials from SEBI stress suitability, risk and goals—not slogans.

If you are comparing fund performance later, compare against the scheme’s stated benchmark in the [factsheet](/blog/how-to-read-a-mutual-fund-factsheet)—not a neighbour’s anecdote.

<Callout type="opinion" title="Our view (opinion)">
RCA is most useful as a behavioural tool: it supports a written plan to invest steadily without obsessing over daily prices. It is a weak slogan if used to ignore fees, risk or product quality.
</Callout>
