India’s personal income-tax framework offers more than one **regime** path for many resident individuals: a structure that allows a wider set of **deductions and exemptions** (often called the **old regime**), and a structure with a different slab design that generally allows **fewer** of those deductions (often called the **new regime**). Labels in payroll software can vary; the legal text for the assessment year you are filing for is what counts.

This guide teaches a **comparison method**. It does not declare a winner for your household.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Compare regimes using **your** income mix and **your** eligible deductions—not a viral “everyone should choose X” post.
- Slab rates, rebates and deduction lists **change**; verify the year on [incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in).
- Employer TDS defaults and Form 16 presentation may assume a regime—confirm what you actually opted for.
- Use [Form 26AS / AIS](/blog/how-to-read-form-26as-and-ais) to cross-check tax already paid.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Start with vocabulary, not slabs

Before opening a calculator:

1. **Gross income components** — salary, house property, capital gains, other sources, as applicable.  
2. **Exemptions** — amounts that do not enter taxable income under stated rules.  
3. **Deductions** — amounts allowed from gross total income under chapter VIA and related provisions when the chosen regime permits them.  
4. **Rebate / cess** — final adjustments under the law for that year.

Our [income-tax basics](/blog/basics-of-income-tax-in-india) article sets the wider map; this page focuses on the regime choice process.

## A practical comparison workflow

### 1. Freeze the year

Write the **financial year / assessment year** (or the new labelling the law uses for that period). Never mix FY 2023–24 slabs with a FY 2026–27 salary.

### 2. List income once

Use the same income figures for both regime calculations. If salary is your main income, collect CTC breakup carefully—see [CTC vs take-home](/blog/ctc-vs-take-home-salary-in-india)—then map taxable salary using current rules.

### 3. List deductions that exist only if you claim them

Under the old-regime style path, common learning examples include investments and expenses that qualify under specific sections when conditions are met. Under the new-regime style path, many of those claims are restricted or unavailable. Build two columns:

- Column A: deductions you **can and do** substantiate under the more deduction-friendly path.  
- Column B: what remains allowed under the alternate path for your year.

Do not invent proofs. No proof usually means no deduction.

### 4. Compute tax both ways with official tools

Prefer the Income Tax Department’s utilities or a careful spreadsheet that uses **official slab tables for that year**. Ignore Instagram “approx tax” cards.

### 5. Choose, then align payroll

If you are a salaried employee, tell your employer the option you intend so monthly [TDS](/blog/what-is-tds-how-to-claim-credit-india) roughly matches. At return filing, follow the rules for that assessment year—including any default regime language in force.

<Callout type="verify">
Default regime for salaried taxpayers, rebate amounts, surcharge thresholds and deduction availability have changed across Budgets. Re-read the official note for the year you are filing; do not rely on last year’s HR email alone.
</Callout>

## Where people make comparison errors

- Counting **HRA / home-loan / 80C-style** benefits in both columns when the chosen path disallows them.  
- Forgetting **employer PF** already reduces take-home but may interact with deduction claims differently than they assume.  
- Comparing only slab percentages while ignoring a large deduction they actually use every year.  
- Ignoring capital gains or other income that may have special rates.  
- Reading [Form 16](/blog/how-to-read-form-16) Part B without checking whether the projected regime matches their plan.

## Illustrative thinking (not numbers to copy)

<Callout type="example" title="Illustrative pattern only">
Household A has few deductions beyond the standard set allowed in both paths; the simpler slab path may look better on paper. Household B has large, documented deductions allowed only under the deduction-friendly path; that path may win even if headline slabs look higher. Your documents decide—not the neighbour’s outcome.
</Callout>

## Documents to keep beside the calculator

1. Latest salary slips and projected annual income  
2. Proofs for every deduction you plan to claim  
3. Form 16 (when available) and AIS/26AS  
4. Official slab / rebate note for the year  
5. Employer declaration form for regime / investments, if applicable  

## Bottom line

Old vs new is a **worksheet problem**, not an identity. Rebuild the comparison when income or deduction patterns change, and always refresh the legal rates for the filing year.
