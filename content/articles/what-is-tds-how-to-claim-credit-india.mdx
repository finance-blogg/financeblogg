**TDS** means **Tax Deducted at Source**. In many everyday situations, the person or organisation paying you income deducts tax and deposits it with the government. You later claim that deducted amount as **tax already paid** when you compute your final tax on the return—subject to the law and correct reporting.

Rules, rates and thresholds change. Treat this as a map of the system, not a calculation of your tax bill. For personalised positions, use a chartered accountant or the official e-filing utilities.

<KeyTakeaways>
- TDS is tax collected **when income is paid** (or credited), by a deductor, under Income Tax provisions.
- You should verify TDS in [Form 26AS / AIS](/blog/how-to-read-form-26as-and-ais) and match salary TDS with [Form 16](/blog/how-to-read-form-16) where relevant.
- Claiming credit generally means reporting the income and the TDS in your return so it reduces tax payable or increases refund—**if** the details match.
- Missing or mismatched TDS is a common reason for refund delays or notices.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Everyday places you may see TDS

Examples (not a complete list):

- Salary (employer deducts).
- Interest on some deposits above thresholds (banks / institutions as applicable).
- Rent, professional fees, contract payments in situations the Act covers.
- Other specified payments where a deductor must deduct.

Whether TDS applies—and at what rate—depends on the section, the amount, your documentation (such as PAN) and current rules.

<Callout type="verify">
TDS rates, threshold amounts and section references change with Finance Acts and notifications. Confirm the current year on [incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in). Keep terminology (financial year / assessment year / tax year) aligned with the law in force when you file.
</Callout>

## How TDS reaches your “credit”

1. Deductor deducts tax and deposits it using your PAN (when required).
2. The deposit is reflected in tax department systems.
3. You see entries in **AIS / Form 26AS** (and related statements).
4. When you file the return, you report income and claim TDS so it counts toward your tax liability.

If the deductor used a wrong PAN or did not deposit, your credit may not appear—even if your payslip showed a deduction. That is why reconciliation matters.

## Practical checks before filing

- Download AIS and Form 26AS for the relevant year.
- Match salary TDS with Form 16 Part A/B as applicable.
- Match interest TDS with bank certificates or statements.
- Follow up with the deductor for missing entries; correcting upstream is often faster than hoping the return “fixes itself”.

## TDS vs advance tax vs self-assessment tax

- **TDS**: someone else deducts when paying you.
- **Advance tax**: you pay during the year when required.
- **Self-assessment tax**: you pay before filing if still short.

All of these can appear in your tax paid profile; they are different pipes into the same destination.
