An **Account Aggregator (AA)** is part of India’s regulated framework for sharing financial information with your **explicit consent**. Instead of emailing PDF statements or giving an app your net-banking password, you can approve a time-bound, purpose-linked request to fetch data from accounts you already hold.

This explainer covers what the system is designed to do. It is not a recommendation to use any particular AA app or lender.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Account Aggregators are **RBI-regulated** entities that handle **consent** and data transfer—they are not your bank.
- You should see **who** is asking, **what** data, **why**, and **for how long** before approving.
- AA sharing does **not** automatically approve a loan or open an investment account.
- Never share OTPs or passwords with anyone who claims to be “activating Account Aggregator” on a call.
</KeyTakeaways>

## The problem AA tries to solve

Lenders, wealth apps and other regulated users often need proof of income, bank balances or holdings. The old path—screenshots, emailed statements, or handing over credentials—is messy and unsafe. AA is meant to move selected financial data through a **consent artefact** you can accept, reject or later revoke under the framework’s rules.

Think of it as a courier with a locked envelope you labelled, not as a new bank account.

## Key roles, in plain language

Official materials describe a few roles. For a customer, three names matter most:

- **Financial Information Provider (FIP):** the institution that holds your data (for example, a bank that holds your savings account).
- **Financial Information User (FIU):** the regulated entity that wants to use your data for a stated purpose (for example, a lender assessing an application).
- **Account Aggregator:** the regulated intermediary that obtains your consent and helps move the permitted data between FIP and FIU without giving the FIU your login password.

Your relationship with your bank for deposits, UPI and statements continues as before. AA sits beside that relationship, not instead of it.

## What a consent request should make clear

Before you tap approve, read the screen slowly:

1. **Name of the FIU** requesting data  
2. **Types of accounts / information** requested  
3. **Purpose** stated on the consent  
4. **Validity period** (from–to dates or duration)  
5. Whether you can **reject** or later **revoke** as the journey allows  

If the screen is vague (“share all finance forever”), treat it as a red flag and exit.

<Callout type="warning" title="Password and OTP safety">
A genuine AA journey should not need your internet-banking password. Banking OTPs are for bank-authenticated steps you initiate. Callers who ask you to “share OTP to link Account Aggregator” are a classic fraud pattern.
</Callout>

## What AA is not

- Not Deposit Insurance. Your deposits still sit with the bank; DICGC coverage rules are unchanged—see [what deposit insurance covers](/blog/what-deposit-insurance-covers-in-india).
- Not a payment app. Sharing a statement snapshot is not the same as [NEFT/RTGS/IMPS/UPI](/blog/neft-rtgs-imps-upi-differences-india).
- Not a guarantee of loan sanction, lower interest or instant KYC success.
- Not a substitute for reading your own [bank statement](/blog/how-to-read-a-bank-statement).

## Practical checks before you approve

1. Confirm you started the journey from the lender or app you intended—not from a random SMS link.  
2. Match the FIU name on the consent with the brand you are dealing with.  
3. Prefer the **minimum** data and duration needed for the purpose.  
4. After the process, watch SMS/email alerts from your bank for unexpected account activity.  
5. Keep nominations and mobile numbers updated so alerts reach you ([nomination guide](/blog/how-bank-account-nomination-works-in-india)).

## When households typically see AA prompts

Common contexts include digital lending journeys, wealth or investment onboarding that asks for holdings, and other regulated use-cases that need verified account information. Exact availability depends on which FIPs and FIUs participate and on product design—confirm inside the official journey, not from a forward.

<Callout type="verify">
Account Aggregator participation, account types supported and consent screens evolve. Prefer RBI communications and the regulated app’s own disclosure over unsigned “AA will share everything” social posts.
</Callout>

## Bottom line

Account Aggregator is a **consent rail** for financial data. Used carefully, it can reduce password sharing and PDF chaos. Used carelessly—approving broad consents or trusting phone “helpers”—it becomes another phishing surface. Read the purpose and the clock on every request.
