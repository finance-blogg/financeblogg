When you invest in a mutual fund, the fund house charges ongoing costs for managing and running the scheme. Those costs are expressed as an **expense ratio**, often discussed as the **Total Expense Ratio (TER)**—a percentage of the fund’s average assets, deducted inside the scheme rather than as a separate bill in your bank account.

Understanding TER helps you read a [factsheet](/blog/how-to-read-a-mutual-fund-factsheet) honestly. It is not a tip to buy or sell any fund.

<KeyTakeaways>
- The expense ratio is an **annualised percentage cost** charged within the scheme.
- You usually **do not pay it as a separate UPI or NEFT**—it reduces the NAV over time.
- [Direct and regular plans](/blog/direct-vs-regular-mutual-fund-plans) of the same scheme can show **different** expense ratios.
- Lower cost helps, but it does not automatically mean better outcomes; risk, mandate and behaviour still matter.
</KeyTakeaways>

## What the ratio is paying for

Broadly, scheme expenses can include investment management fees, registrar and transfer agent costs, custody, audit, marketing and distribution-related costs (where applicable), and other permitted expenses under SEBI rules. The exact mix is a regulatory and accounting topic; investors mainly need to know that **costs are real** and disclosed.

SEBI regulates how mutual funds charge expenses. Caps and structures have changed over time—verify current limits on SEBI/AMFI materials rather than memorising an old number from a blog.

## How it shows up for you

Suppose a fund’s TER is quoted as a percentage per year. That does not mean ₹X leaves your savings account each month. Instead, expenses are accounted for in the scheme, and the **NAV** you see is after those costs (along with market moves).

<Callout type="example" title="Illustrative maths only">
If someone invests ₹1,00,000 and imagines a flat 1% annual expense with no market move, the cost idea is about ₹1,000 over a year—before tax and before real market gains or losses. Markets are never flat; this is only to show scale.
</Callout>

## Where to read it

- Scheme **factsheet** and **key information memorandum (KIM)** / offer documents.
- AMC websites and app disclosures for the specific plan (direct vs regular).
- Compare the same scheme’s plans carefully; labels matter.

When you study a scheme for learning purposes, expense ratio is one column—not the whole report card. Exit loads and lock-ins (if any) are separate from TER—read them in the same offer documents.

## Myths to avoid

- “Zero expense means zero risk.” Market-linked funds still carry market risk.
- “Highest TER means best manager.” Cost and skill are not the same thing.
- “TER is the only fee that ever exists.” Exit loads, taxes and your own behaviour also affect outcomes.
- “Past return after expenses guarantees the future.” It does not.
