**Advance tax** is income tax you pay **during the financial year** in instalments when your estimated tax liability for the year is not fully covered by tax already deducted or collected at source. It is the “pay as you earn” mechanism for people whose income is not only salary with adequate [TDS](/blog/what-is-tds-how-to-claim-credit-india).

This explainer is educational. It is not a calculation service or filing advice for your return.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Advance tax applies when **estimated annual tax** exceeds the threshold set in the law for that year—after considering TDS/TCS.
- Payment is typically in **scheduled instalments** during the year (dates and percentages are notified in the Act/rules).
- Salary-only earners with correct TDS often have little or no advance tax; freelancers, business owners, and those with large other income should estimate carefully.
- Cross-check credits in [Form 26AS / AIS](/blog/how-to-read-form-26as-and-ais) before you assume a shortfall or surplus.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Where advance tax sits in the tax calendar

Think of the year’s tax as a bill estimated in advance:

1. Estimate total taxable income under the regime you plan to use — see [basics of income tax](/blog/basics-of-income-tax-in-india) and [old vs new regime](/blog/old-vs-new-tax-regime-india-how-to-compare).  
2. Estimate tax on that income.  
3. Subtract TDS/TCS expected to be reported.  
4. If the remaining tax liability crosses the legal threshold, pay **advance tax** in instalments.  
5. After year-end, file the return; pay **self-assessment tax** if anything is still short.

Advance tax is not a separate “type of income tax.” It is **timing** of payment of the same income-tax liability.

## Who typically needs to look closely

Situations where advance tax often becomes relevant:

- Business or professional income with little TDS  
- Capital gains or other irregular income  
- Interest, rent, or freelance receipts where TDS is absent or insufficient  
- Multiple employers / late TDS corrections mid-year  

Many salaried employees with a single employer and correct TDS may not need separate advance-tax payments. That is a **pattern**, not a personal clearance—your Form 16, salary structure, and other income decide.

## Instalments: concept without memorising a viral table

The Income-tax framework specifies **due dates** and **minimum percentages** of estimated tax to be paid by those dates for the financial year. Missing instalments can attract interest provisions under the Act.

Because percentages and dates are easy to get wrong from memory—and can be amended—treat any social-media table as suspect until you match it to the Income Tax Department’s current material on [incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in).

<Callout type="example" title="Illustrative estimation sketch">
You estimate tax for the year at ₹1,20,000 and expect TDS of ₹40,000. Remaining ≈ ₹80,000. If that remaining amount exceeds the advance-tax threshold then in force, you schedule instalments toward the ₹80,000 during the year and reconcile at return time. Figures are illustrative only—not your assessment.
</Callout>

<Callout type="verify">
Advance-tax thresholds, instalment due dates, percentage schedules, and interest provisions are set under the Income-tax Act/rules and can change with Finance Acts or notifications. Confirm on incometax.gov.in for the relevant assessment year before paying or advising others.
</Callout>

## Practical habits that reduce surprises

1. **Re-estimate mid-year** when a large gain, bonus, or business swing happens.  
2. **Match AIS/26AS** so you do not double-count or miss TDS — [Form 26AS and AIS guide](/blog/how-to-read-form-26as-and-ais).  
3. **Keep challan details** for every advance-tax payment with the correct assessment year and major/minor head as required.  
4. **Do not confuse GST** with income-tax advance tax; GST has its own compliance calendar—see [how to understand GST Council decisions](/blog/how-to-understand-gst-council-decisions-for-households) for household-level GST literacy (different tax).  

## Advance tax vs self-assessment tax vs TDS

| Mechanism | When it usually happens | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TDS/TCS | During the year at source | Prepaid tax reported by deductors |
| Advance tax | During the year by taxpayer | Prepaid tax when TDS is not enough |
| Self-assessment tax | Before filing the return | Clears remaining liability |

All of these feed into the same final tax computation on the return.

## Bottom line

Advance tax is how India collects income tax **in instalments during the year** when your estimated liability is not fully covered by TDS/TCS. If your income is simple and TDS is correct, you may rarely touch it; if your income is irregular, estimate early, verify credits, and follow current official due dates—not forwarded charts.
