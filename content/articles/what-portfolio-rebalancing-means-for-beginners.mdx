You might choose a mix such as “more growth assets, some safer assets” based on your goals and [risk tolerance](/blog/what-is-risk-tolerance-for-beginner-investors). Markets then move at different speeds. The slice that rose faster becomes a larger share of your portfolio—even if you bought nothing new. **Rebalancing** means adjusting holdings so the mix returns closer to the plan you originally set.

Rebalancing is a risk-management habit. It is not a promise of higher returns.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Asset weights drift when markets move; rebalancing restores your chosen [asset allocation](/blog/what-asset-allocation-means-for-beginners).
- Common approaches: review on a **calendar** (for example yearly) or when weights move beyond a **threshold**.
- Selling can trigger taxes and costs such as [exit loads](/blog/what-is-exit-load-in-mutual-funds-india); buying needs cash or fresh SIPs.
- Rebalancing can feel uncomfortable because it often trims what recently did well.
- No schedule guarantees better performance than “do nothing”—the goal is staying aligned with your risk plan.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Why portfolios drift

Suppose, for teaching only, you aimed for 60% growth-oriented assets and 40% relatively stable assets. After a strong year for growth assets, the split might become 70/30. Your **risk** is now higher than the plan—even though your “strategy” never changed on paper.

Drift also happens the other way: after a fall in growth assets, the portfolio may become more conservative than you intended, which can matter if your goal still needs long-term growth.

[Diversification](/blog/what-diversification-means-for-beginner-investors) spreads risk across holdings; rebalancing keeps the *weights* from quietly rewriting your plan.

## Two simple rebalancing methods

### Calendar rebalancing

You review on a fixed schedule—often annually or semi-annually—and adjust back toward targets. It is easy to remember and pairs well with a [quarterly or annual money review](/blog/how-to-do-a-quarterly-money-review-in-india).

### Threshold rebalancing

You act only when a weight moves more than a set gap (for example five percentage points) from target. Quiet markets mean fewer trades; turbulent markets mean more attention.

Neither method is universally “best”. Consistency and cost awareness matter more than cleverness.

## Ways to rebalance without drama

1. **Redirect new investing** — put fresh SIPs or deposits into the underweight sleeve until weights normalise.  
2. **Sell and buy** — trim the overweight sleeve and add to the underweight one.  
3. **Hybrid** — use new money first; sell only what you still need to correct.

Using new money can reduce taxable sales, but it is slower when drift is large or you are not adding cash.

## Costs and frictions to respect

- **Taxes** — selling profitable units or shares can create taxable gains under current law; rules differ by asset type and holding period.  
- **Exit loads and spreads** — mutual funds may charge an exit load if you redeem too early; see [exit load explained](/blog/what-is-exit-load-in-mutual-funds-india).  
- **Behaviour** — trimming winners feels “wrong” even when it restores your plan.  
- **Over-trading** — monthly tinkering can raise costs without improving discipline.

Rebalancing should be boring. If it feels like market timing, pause and return to your written allocation.

## What rebalancing does not do

- It does not eliminate losses.  
- It does not replace an emergency fund or debt plan.  
- It does not tell you *which* fund or stock to own.  
- It does not override a mismatched goal—if the original allocation was too aggressive, fix the plan first.

[Compounding](/blog/what-is-compounding-in-investing) still needs time; rebalancing is about the path of risk along the way.

## A beginner checklist

1. Write target percentages and the goal date.  
2. Choose calendar, threshold, or both.  
3. Note where each holding lives (fund house, demat, bank).  
4. Estimate tax and exit-load impact before selling.  
5. Record what you did and why, so next year’s self understands the decision.

## Limitations

All percentages in this article are illustrative. Suitable allocations depend on goals, horizon, liabilities and personal risk capacity. Tax law and product costs change. This is education, not personalised investment advice. For regulated investor education, use [investor.sebi.gov.in](https://investor.sebi.gov.in).

## Bottom line

Rebalancing is how you stop market moves from silently changing your risk. Pick a simple rule, respect taxes and fees, and use new contributions when you can. The win is staying on the plan you chose—not beating a benchmark every quarter.
