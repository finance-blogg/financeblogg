A monthly [budget](/blog/how-to-create-a-monthly-budget-in-india) tracks what came in and went out. A **quarterly money review** steps back: are goals still funded, is debt under control, and is paperwork still accurate? Ninety days is long enough for patterns to show and short enough to catch problems before they compound.

This is a household process guide. It is not a tip to buy, sell or switch any product.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Pick one fixed date each quarter and use the **same checklist** every time.
- Review **cash buffer, cash flow, debt, goals and insurance/paperwork**—in that order.
- Record numbers, not opinions; pair this with a simple [net-worth snapshot](/blog/how-to-track-net-worth-in-india) once or twice a year.
- Separating “review” from “trade” keeps the meeting short and less stressful.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Why quarterly works better than “whenever”

Daily checking of investment apps often creates noise. Waiting a full year can hide EMI stress, a thinning emergency fund or an expired nomination. A quarterly cadence sits in the middle: enough data for decisions about habits, without pretending markets need a weekly vote.

Put the review on a calendar the way you would a rent reminder. One hour is enough for most households.

## Step 1: Cash and emergency buffer

Open bank and liquid balances you actually use. Ask only:

1. Is the [emergency fund](/blog/emergency-fund-how-much-should-you-save) still in the range you chose?
2. Did any large outflow this quarter look one-off or recurring?
3. Are auto-debits still going to the right accounts?

You are measuring **runway**, not chasing the highest savings rate of the week.

## Step 2: Cash flow reality check

Pull three months of statements or your budget app export. Compare planned categories with actual spend. Look for:

- subscriptions you forgot;
- EMI + rent + school fees crowding out savings;
- income that arrived late or irregularly (bonuses, overtime, freelancing).

If take-home is confusing, revisit [CTC vs take-home](/blog/ctc-vs-take-home-salary-in-india) before blaming “discipline”.

<Callout type="example" title="Illustrative review note">
“Groceries averaged ₹X above plan for three months; dining was within plan.” That sentence is useful. “We are bad with money” is not a review finding.
</Callout>

## Step 3: Debt and credit obligations

List every EMI and revolving balance. For each loan, note outstanding principal, rate type if you know it, and next due date. For cards, note statement balance versus minimum due—paying only the minimum is expensive education, not a strategy.

Check that autopay covers at least the full statement where you intend to stay debt-free on revolving credit. Then glance at your credit report once in a while so errors do not sit for years; see [how credit scores work](/blog/how-credit-scores-work-in-india).

## Step 4: Goals and upcoming cash needs

Rewrite [financial goals](/blog/how-to-set-financial-goals-in-india) in one line each: purpose, rough target month/year, and whether funding is on track. School fees, insurance renewals, travel and tax payments often break budgets because they are predictable but not monthly.

Move known annual costs into a sinking line item in the next quarter’s budget rather than hoping the salary month will “feel rich”.

## Step 5: Insurance and paperwork

Confirm:

- health and life cover still match household size and loans;
- nominees on bank, PF and investments are current ([bank nomination explainer](/blog/how-bank-account-nomination-works-in-india));
- KYC / email / phone on important accounts still work.

You do not need to rewrite every policy each quarter—only verify nothing critical expired or drifted.

## What not to do in the same sitting

- Do not turn the review into a stock or fund shopping spree.
- Do not change five products at once because a reel said so.
- Do not skip writing the numbers down; memory is a poor ledger.

<Callout type="opinion" title="Our view (opinion)">
A boring one-page note—“buffer OK, EMI share X%, next big cash need in Month Y”—beats a colourful dashboard you never reopen.
</Callout>

## A one-page quarterly template

Copy this into a notes app:

1. Date of review  
2. Emergency buffer (weeks/months of expenses)  
3. Top three spending overruns  
4. Total EMI / essential outgo as a share of take-home (your estimate)  
5. Goals on track / behind  
6. Paperwork actions (nominee, KYC, renewal dates)  
7. One habit change for next quarter  

Close the laptop when the page is full. That is a complete review.
