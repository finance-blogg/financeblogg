Every SEBI-registered mutual fund scheme is sold in at least two plans: **direct** and **regular**. The portfolio manager, the stocks or bonds, the exit load rules and the riskometer are the same scheme. The difference is **who you bought it through**, and therefore **how much the fund deducts each year** as expenses.

If you already read our introduction to [SIPs and mutual funds](/blog/understanding-sip-and-mutual-fund-investing), this article is the cost layer. It does not tell you to fire a distributor or to pick a scheme.

<KeyTakeaways>
- A **direct plan** is bought from the AMC or a platform that offers direct plans, without a distributor commission in the expense ratio.
- A **regular plan** is bought through a distributor. The fund pays a commission from the scheme’s expenses, so the **total expense ratio is higher**.
- The extra cost is taken from the fund continuously. You do not see a separate bill.
- Over long periods, a small annual gap **compounds**.
- Regular is not “fake” and direct is not “secret”. The question is whether you are receiving advice or service that is worth the extra annual cost.
</KeyTakeaways>

## How to see which plan you hold

Look at the scheme name on your statement, app, or Consolidated Account Statement (CAS):

- Direct plans include the word **Direct** in the plan name.
- Regular plans may say **Regular**, or simply omit “Direct”.

Also check the **NAV**. Direct and regular plans of the same scheme publish different NAVs because expenses differ. A higher NAV on the direct plan is not a different portfolio; it is usually the same investments minus a smaller expense drag.

If you invest through a bank relationship manager, many workplace platforms, or a neighbourhood distributor, you may be in regular by default. If you invest on the AMC’s website and chose direct, you should see Direct in the name. Do not guess; open the latest CAS.

## What the expense ratio is doing

The **total expense ratio (TER)** is the annual cost of running the scheme, deducted from the fund’s assets. It includes management fees, operating costs and, in a regular plan, distribution costs. SEBI caps how high TER can go. Caps are not the same as what you actually pay; two funds in the same category can sit at different levels under the cap.

Because the deduction is daily and built into the NAV, a 0.8% gap does not show up as “₹80 missing this year” on a ₹10,000 SIP in a way that feels dramatic. Over 15 years of monthly investing, the same gap is the difference between two very different maturity piles, all else equal.

<Callout type="example" title="Same return before costs, different TER">
Hypothetical illustration, not a projection. Assume ₹10,000 a month for 20 years and a **made-up** market return of 8% a year **before** expenses.

- At an annual cost of 0.5%, more of that 8% stays in the NAV.
- At an annual cost of 1.5%, one percentage point is eaten every year, including on money that was invested in year one.

The extra 1% is not taken once. It is taken again on last year’s growth. Real returns will not be a smooth 8%, and they can be negative. The only point of the example is compounding of costs.
</Callout>

All else is not always equal. A regular plan does not change the fund manager’s stock picks. It does change your net return if the portfolio is the same.

## What you are paying for in a regular plan

A distributor with an AMFI Registration Number (ARN) is paid from the scheme. In return, you might get help with KYC, form filling, reminders, and someone to call. Some distributors do real work. Some only collect the trail commission after a one-time signature.

A **SEBI-registered investment adviser** is a different role. Advisers are paid by you (a fee) for advice, and the product can still be direct. Do not confuse a sales conversation with advice just because it felt personal.

<Callout type="note" title="Switching is a transaction">
Moving from regular to direct is usually a **redemption and a fresh purchase**, or a switch if the AMC allows it. That can:

- start a new holding period for tax;
- trigger **exit load** if you are still inside the load window;
- in ELSS, run into the lock-in on each SIP instalment.

Check the tax and load before you “just switch”. Our [income tax basics](/blog/basics-of-income-tax-in-india) piece flags that rates change; confirm the current capital-gains rules before redeeming.
</Callout>

## Direct plans still have costs and risks

Direct does not mean free. The fund still charges a TER. Direct also does not mean better stock picking. A high-cost regular plan in a strong process can still beat a cheap direct plan in a weak one over a given period, though you cannot know that in advance, and past performance is not a reliable forecast.

Direct also means **you** must:

- complete KYC;
- pick a scheme category that matches the goal’s horizon;
- read the Scheme Information Document, riskometer and exit load;
- keep nominees and bank details updated;
- avoid chasing last year’s return chart.

If you would not do that work, paying for a competent human may be rational. Paying for a logo on a form is not.

## A short comparison

| | Direct | Regular |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How you buy | AMC or a direct-enabled platform | Distributor (ARN) |
| TER | Lower, no distributor commission in the plan | Higher, includes distribution costs |
| Portfolio | Same scheme | Same scheme |
| NAV | Different (usually higher than regular) | Different |
| Suitable when | You can execute and review yourself, or you pay an adviser separately | You want the distributor’s service and know what it costs |

Neither plan is a guarantee. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks. [Diversification](/blog/what-diversification-means-for-beginner-investors) and time horizon still matter more than the word Direct on the folio.

## Practical checks

1. Download the latest CAS from the KRA or your registrar emails and search for `Direct`.
2. Look up the scheme on AMFI or the AMC site and compare **Direct** and **Regular** TER and NAV.
3. If someone is advising you, ask whether they are a **distributor**, an **adviser**, or both, and how they are paid.
4. Verify an ARN on AMFI’s site and an IA registration on SEBI’s site.
5. Do not provide OTPs or complete KYC on a forwarded link.

<Callout type="opinion">
We think the useful question is not “is regular a scam?” It is “what am I receiving every year for this extra TER?” If the answer is a one-time form and silence, the regular plan is an expensive habit. If the answer is a documented plan you follow, the cost is a fee with a messy invoice. Either way, know which plan you hold.
</Callout>
