**House Rent Allowance (HRA)** is a salary component many employers pay to help with rent. For tax, the question is whether part of that HRA is **exempt** from income tax when you actually pay rent for a home you occupy. The exemption, when available under the law for your chosen regime and year, is usually the *least* of several calculated amounts—not automatically the full HRA in your CTC.

This guide explains the idea for salaried readers in India. It is educational, not a filing service or personalised tax advice.

<KeyTakeaways>
- HRA exemption (when allowed) generally needs: HRA in salary, actual rent paid, and that you do not claim the same benefit twice in forbidden ways—confirm current law.
- Employers often need **rent receipts / lease** and sometimes the landlord’s PAN above notified thresholds.
- Metro vs non-metro classification can change one of the formula legs—definitions follow the tax rules for that year.
- Under some years’ **new tax regime** rules, many salary exemptions including HRA may be unavailable; compare regimes carefully.
- Match HRA claims with [Form 16](/blog/how-to-read-form-16), payslips and [Form 26AS / AIS](/blog/how-to-read-form-26as-and-ais).
</KeyTakeaways>

## HRA on the payslip vs HRA exemption

Your payslip may show HRA as a fixed allowance. That line is **income** unless an exemption applies. Exemption is computed using rules in the Income-tax Act and related notifications for the financial year.

If you live in your own house and pay no rent, HRA exemption generally does not apply in the usual salaried sense—even if HRA appears in the CTC. (Other deductions related to home loans are a separate topic and also depend on regime and year.)

## The exemption idea in plain language

When the law allows HRA exemption, tax officers and payroll software typically compare amounts such as:

- the actual HRA received;
- rent paid minus a fraction of salary (as defined in the rules);
- a percentage of salary that depends on whether you live in a notified metro city or elsewhere.

The **exempt amount is the lowest** of those legs. Everything above that remains taxable HRA.

<Callout type="verify" title="Formula details change—and regime matters">
Percentages, metro lists and whether HRA exemption is available at all depend on the Finance Act rules for the year and on whether you opt for a regime that allows the exemption. Confirm on [incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in) or with a qualified tax professional before you file.
</Callout>

## Documents people commonly keep

Practices vary by employer, but these are often requested:

- monthly **rent receipts** with landlord details and revenue stamp where still relevant under local practice;
- **rental agreement**;
- landlord **PAN** when rent for the year crosses the threshold notified in the rules;
- bank proof of rent transfer (increasingly useful if questions arise).

Paying large rent in cash without paperwork is how claims get stuck in scrutiny.

## Special situations to read carefully

- **Living with parents** — some employees pay rent to parents who own the house. This can be valid only when the arrangement is genuine, documented, and the parent declares rental income as required. Artificial paperwork is a compliance risk.  
- **Shared flats** — claim only your share of rent, with clear splitting.  
- **City change mid-year** — metro vs non-metro legs may need month-wise care.  
- **Work-from-home in another city** — follow the rules for the place of residence you claim; do not invent a convenient city.

## How HRA interacts with tax regimes

India’s [old vs new tax regime](/blog/old-vs-new-tax-regime-india-how-to-compare) choice can decide whether HRA exemption is on the table. A lower headline slab rate may coexist with fewer exemptions. Run both scenarios with *your* rent, HRA and other deductions—not a generic social-media table.

CTC versus take-home differences are explained in [CTC vs take-home salary](/blog/ctc-vs-take-home-salary-in-india); HRA is one reason two people with similar CTC keep different amounts after tax.

## Claiming through employer vs at return filing

Many employees submit proofs to payroll so TDS is lower during the year. If proofs were missing, you may still need to compute correctly in the return—subject to law—and fix excess TDS via refund mechanics. Either path requires honest numbers.

## Limitations

Tax rates, exemption formulae, documentation thresholds and regime features change with each Finance Act and CBDT guidance. This article does not compute your exemption and is not advice. Use official utilities, Form 16, AIS and a qualified professional when amounts are material.

## Bottom line

HRA exemption, when available, is the lowest of several legal calculations—not a free pass on the full allowance. Keep rent proofs, respect regime rules for the year, and reconcile payslips with Form 16 before you file.
