**Portability** in health insurance means moving from one insurer (or one retail policy) to another while seeking credit for the time you already spent in a previous policy—especially for **waiting periods**. Done well, portability can let you switch products without restarting every waiting clock from zero. Done late or with incomplete papers, you can lose continuity and face fresh waits.

This article explains the idea for Indian retail health cover. It is not a recommendation to port or to choose any insurer.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Portability is about carrying **continuity benefits** (notably waiting-period credit) when you change insurer or product under applicable IRDAI rules.
- Apply within the timelines your current and new insurers publish—often linked to the renewal window; late applications are a common failure point.
- The new insurer still **underwrites** you; portability is not an automatic guarantee of identical terms, [sum insured](/blog/what-is-sum-insured-in-health-insurance-india) or price.
- Keep policy schedules, claim history and renewal notices ready.
- Compare benefits, exclusions, [copay/deductible](/blog/health-insurance-deductible-copay-india) and network hospitals *before* you start the port request.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Why people consider porting

Common reasons include dissatisfaction with claim experience, desire for a different room-rent structure, family floater redesign, or a product that better matches a new life stage. None of those reasons outweigh reading the new policy wording. A prettier brochure that restarts waiting periods—or shrinks coverage—can be a step backwards.

First understand [waiting periods and exclusions](/blog/health-insurance-waiting-periods-and-exclusions), then decide whether continuity is worth the switch.

## What may carry over

Under the portability framework supervised by IRDAI, eligible policyholders can seek credit for the period already served toward waiting periods when moving to a similar retail health product, subject to conditions. Details such as which waits credit fully, how [no-claim bonus](/blog/what-is-no-claim-bonus-in-health-insurance-india) is treated, and how sum-insured increases are handled depend on regulations and the receiving insurer’s underwriting.

<Callout type="verify" title="Confirm the current IRDAI rules">
Portability procedures, forms and timelines are updated through IRDAI regulations and circulars. Before you act, read the latest consumer guidance on [irdai.gov.in](https://www.irdai.gov.in) and the portability section of both insurers’ websites.
</Callout>

## What usually does not mean “same policy, new logo”

Portability is not a clone button. The new insurer may:

- accept, modify or decline based on medical history;
- offer a different premium for the same sum insured;
- apply product-specific sub-limits, copays or disease-wise caps;
- treat an increase in sum insured with fresh waiting periods on the *increased* portion, as per rules and policy terms.

Always read the proposal benefit illustration and policy wording, not only the sales pitch.

## Typical process (high level)

Exact steps vary, but the educational outline looks like this:

1. **Decide and compare** using a structured checklist ([compare policies](/blog/how-to-compare-health-insurance-policies)).  
2. **Start the portability request** with the new insurer within the allowed window relative to your renewal.  
3. **Share documents**: previous policy copies, proposal forms, claim details if any, ID/KYC as asked.  
4. **Underwriting** by the new insurer.  
5. **Decision** — accept on offered terms, negotiate clarity in writing, or stay with the existing insurer if the offer is poor.  
6. **Break-free continuity** — ensure there is no unintended gap in cover between old policy end and new policy start.

## Mistakes that break continuity

- Applying after the portability window has closed.  
- Letting the old policy lapse before the new one is in force.  
- Hiding past claims or medical history—non-disclosure can haunt future claims.  
- Assuming every rider and every waiting period ports unchanged.  
- Raising sum insured sharply without understanding waits on the enhanced cover.

## Portability vs simply buying a new policy

Buying a fresh policy without porting usually means **new waiting periods** from day one for the new contract. Portability exists specifically so continuous, eligible cover can carry credit. If you have already served long waits for pre-existing conditions, casually dropping cover to “buy cheaper elsewhere” can be expensive in disguise.

## Limitations

This explainer summarises concepts for education. IRDAI regulations, insurer SOPs, medical underwriting guidelines and product wordings control real outcomes. Premiums, networks and exclusions change. FinanceBlogg does not sell insurance or earn commission on portability decisions. For disputes, use the insurer’s grievance process and IRDAI’s consumer channels as applicable.

## Bottom line

Health insurance portability helps you switch insurers while seeking credit for waiting periods you already served—if you apply on time, disclose honestly and accept that underwriting still applies. Compare the full product, protect continuity of cover, and verify steps on official IRDAI and insurer pages before you move.
