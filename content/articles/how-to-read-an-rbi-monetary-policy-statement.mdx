Every few weeks, headlines say the RBI has “held rates”, “cut rates” or “surprised the market”. The underlying document is usually a **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** resolution and a Governor’s statement. Those pages are shorter than the TV debate, and they are the part that is official.

This explainer is about how to read that packet. It will not predict the next decision, and it will not tell you whether to prepay a loan.

<KeyTakeaways>
- The MPC’s headline tool for this purpose is the **policy repo rate**, plus a **stance** (such as withdrawing accommodation, or remaining open to action either way).
- India’s inflation target is set in terms of **CPI** inflation, not the Sensex.
- A repo change does **not** rewrite your EMI the same afternoon. Transmission to lending and deposit rates takes time and depends on whether your loan is floating, and on the benchmark it uses.
- Minutes and the inflation forecast matter more than a single adjective in a ticker.
- For personal decisions, pair this with [how loan interest rates work](/blog/understanding-loan-interest-rates) and [how inflation affects savings](/blog/how-inflation-affects-your-savings).
</KeyTakeaways>

## What the MPC is

The Monetary Policy Committee is a statutory body that meets on a published schedule, typically every two months, and votes on the policy rate. The resolution records the vote. Members can disagree; that disagreement is later visible in the **minutes**.

The legal job, under India’s inflation-targeting framework, is to keep **Consumer Price Index (CPI)** inflation close to the target set by the government (in recent years, 4 per cent with a tolerance band of ±2 percentage points). Growth matters in the statement; the target the committee is judged on is inflation.

<Callout type="verify">
The numerical inflation target and the committee’s composition are notified by the government for a period. If you are citing the 4% ±2% band, confirm it is still the notified target on the [RBI monetary policy page](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMonetaryPolicy.aspx) for the date you care about.
</Callout>

## The documents, in order

On decision day, start here, on RBI’s site, not on a screenshot:

1. **MPC resolution** — the voted decision on the repo rate and the stance, in a few pages.
2. **Governor’s statement** — the explanation: inflation, growth, risks, and what the committee is watching.
3. **Monetary Policy Report** (when issued) — longer forecasts and charts.
4. **Minutes** (published later) — how each member argued. This is where a “unanimous hold” versus a split vote becomes clear.

If a forward on WhatsApp does not match the resolution’s wording, the resolution wins.

## The lines that actually change policy

**Repo rate.** The rate at which RBI injects overnight rupees against government securities under the liquidity framework. It is the centre of the interest-rate corridor. When people say “RBI rates”, they usually mean this number.

**Standing facilities and the corridor.** Around the repo sit facilities that stop money-market rates from wandering too far. You do not need the full jargon to read the statement; you do need to know that the repo is the reference, not your home-loan rate itself.

**Stance.** Language such as “remain focused on withdrawal of accommodation” or “change the stance to neutral” is a signal about the **direction of travel**, not a second repo number. Markets trade on it because it hints at the next few meetings.

**Liquidity and CRR.** Sometimes the committee or the RBI also changes cash reserve requirements or describes surplus or deficit liquidity. That can matter for banks’ funding; it is a separate lever from the repo.

**Forecasts.** The statement usually includes inflation and growth projections for coming quarters. A hold with a sharply higher inflation forecast is not the same story as a hold with inflation sliding toward 4%.

## What typically does not change that day

- The **DICGC** cover on your deposits. That is a different institution and a different circular. See [what deposit insurance covers](/blog/what-deposit-insurance-covers-in-india).
- Your **fixed-rate** loan, until the reset date in the contract, if any.
- Mutual fund NAVs as a direct official action. Markets may move; that is price discovery, not an RBI debit from your folio.
- Income-tax slabs. Those come from the Budget and the tax law, not the MPC.

<Callout type="example" title="A 25 basis-point change on a floating loan">
Illustrative mechanics, not a forecast.

A **basis point** is 0.01 percentage points. A 25 bps repo cut is 0.25%. If a floating personal or home loan is linked to an external benchmark that follows policy rates, the **benchmark** may move, and then your spread is added. Lenders have timelines and floors in the agreement. Your EMI might fall, or tenure might fall, depending on the reset rules. None of that is instantaneous just because a press conference ended.
</Callout>

## How this reaches your EMI and FD

**Floating-rate retail loans** in India are often linked to an external benchmark, such as a repo-linked rate or a Treasury-bill rate, plus a spread the bank sets. A policy cut is a reason to watch the benchmark and your next reset circular, not to assume the EMI already changed.

**Deposits** are a pricing decision by each bank. Card rates on new [FDs and RDs](/blog/how-recurring-deposits-work) may move with the cycle, slowly or not at all. An existing FD keeps the booked rate until maturity.

**Transmission** is the word RBI uses for this pass-through. It is uneven. Do not take a TV guest’s “EMIs will drop from tomorrow” as a fact.

If you are considering using surplus cash to close a loan, that is a [prepayment decision](/blog/how-loan-prepayment-and-foreclosure-work), not a monetary-policy trade.

## A one-page reading checklist

When the statement drops, write six things on a note:

1. Repo rate: old vs new.
2. Stance: copied as a quote, not paraphrased by a channel.
3. Vote: unanimous or split (from the resolution; details in minutes later).
4. CPI inflation: latest print they cite, and their forecast.
5. Growth: the number they are using, and the risk they flag (monsoon, oil, global rates).
6. Any extra action: CRR, OMO, or a scheme they name.

Then stop. You do not need a view on the rupee in the next hour to be a competent reader.

<Callout type="warning" title="Policy day scams">
Announcements attract fake broker “tips” and phishing pages that mimic RBI. RBI does not message you on Telegram to recover money. Open rbi.org.in yourself. Do not log in to net banking from a link in a rate-cut forward.
</Callout>

## Connecting the statement to household inflation

The CPI basket is not your personal grocery bag. If food is your large spend, your inflation can run hotter than the headline. The MPC still targets the official CPI. For household planning, use the statement to understand the **direction of rates and inflation**, then keep your [budget](/blog/how-to-create-a-monthly-budget-in-india) in rupees you actually spend.

<Callout type="opinion">
We think most readers need one habit: open the resolution, copy the repo and the stance, and ignore adjectives. “Bold”, “dovish” and “the street expected” are commentary. Your loan agreement and your CPI-relevant spending are the household documents. Keep those two stacked next to the PDF, not next to a poll of analysts.
</Callout>

Primary source, every time: the [RBI monetary policy page](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMonetaryPolicy.aspx). If a number is not in that PDF, it is not “what RBI announced”.
